# Understand customers

Find out why the people the user wants to reach would act now, and what stops them. A video makes someone act when four things hold: **need** (this is my problem), **value** (this product solves it), **trust** (there is reason to believe it) and **action** (I know where and how). Research only what is still unknown for those four; the result is a hypothesis for content, not proof that anything will convert.

## Start from what the user has

Reuse the conversation and, when personal memory is connected, saved product facts (read_memory search). If the open question still needs customer material the conversation lacks, ask once, in one message, only for the kinds missing: reviews, customer questions or support logs, return or cancellation reasons, survey answers, results from past content. Skip the question when what the user gave already answers it. Their own customers' words outrank anything public. If they have nothing or decline, continue with public sources and say the note is built without their customer data.

Do not ask for what you can look up, and do not ask about price, stock or channels unless the goal depends on them.

## Fill the gaps from public sources

Pick sources by the question still open; do not tour every source. Use only the host's web search and pages that open without signing in: no scripts, store or review APIs, bulk paging or the user's logged-in sessions. When pages keep failing, stop as researching-on-the-web does and say what stayed unread.

- **Why people choose or hesitate:** reviews of the product and of close alternatives for the same use. Prefer verified purchases and read both high and low ratings and recent ones. A review summary is a lead; quote the original review.
- **What can be promised and shown:** the product page, manuals, test results or demos the user can film. A claim with no proof stays a claim to verify.
- **What customers compare against:** competitor product pages and the offers on them, and ads only through links the user shares.
- **What blocks the last step:** the purchase or sign-up path the user describes or shares (price shown, shipping, required steps).

Short-video comments are finding-comments' source. Use comments already in the conversation under the evidence rules below; when the need or hesitation is still open, say that comment research could add evidence. Do not search for videos or comments here.

## Keep evidence honest

- Quote original words with their source link; keep translations separate. Leave out names, emails, order numbers, phone numbers and other details that identify a customer.
- **Confirmed** means a source says it: the user's data, a review, a page. Applying it to the user's target customers, or anything inferred, is a **hypothesis**. Mark the quote and the inference separately.
- Reviewers and commenters are not necessarily target customers or buyers. Likes, views and star counts show reaction within that source, never purchases or market share.
- Separate buyers' reasons from non-buyers' hesitations when the source tells you which; otherwise say it is unknown.
- When a source fails or returns little, say so; do not pad with invented problems.

## Return a research note

Keep it to one screen, in the user's language:

1. **Who and when:** the customer and the situation of use, with sources.
2. **Need / Value / Trust / Action:** for each, what is known, the strongest evidence (quote, source, confirmed or hypothesis) and the open gap.
3. **Main hesitation to resolve:** the one or two blockers the content should answer, and what would prove the answer on camera.
4. **For the next step:** the customer situation and hesitation to use when searching for reference videos, and the product claims a brief could make. Public-source claims are claims to confirm with the user; a brief uses product facts only from the user. Name what still needs the user's input.

Do not write the brief, search videos or plan the campaign here; reference search belongs to finding-references and the brief to writing-content-briefs.

## Anti-Patterns

- Treating comments, reviews or views as proof of what drives purchases.
- Running every source when the user's own data already answers the question.
- Presenting an inferred hesitation as a customer finding without the hypothesis mark.
- Building the note from the product page alone and calling it customer research.
