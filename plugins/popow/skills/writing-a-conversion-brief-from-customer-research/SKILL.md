# Write a conversion brief from customer research

The user wants viewers to take an action and has not yet settled what to say or which video to follow. Finish with one shootable brief whose message answers a real customer hesitation. Take only the steps the conversation still needs; running every step is not the goal.

## Read what the user gave

Read five things from the request and the conversation before the first step. Do not ask for one the user already gave.

| What to read | Example from one request |
|---|---|
| Product or service | "Popow, a tool that finds ad references" |
| Viewer | "a beauty brand marketer planning the next AI ad for a moisturizer" |
| Action the video should lead to | "start a paid subscription" |
| Selling points, or the hesitation to answer | "find the hand movement and close-up for the ad in minutes" |
| Competitors or videos to look at | "include UGC for competing ad tools" |

The examples belong to that request; use what this user says. The viewer and the selling point together are enough to search for references. When either is missing, step 1 fills it.

## Steps

1. **Why customers would act or hesitate:** follow understanding-customers. Skip it when the user gave the viewer and a selling point, or the hesitation to answer. Carry forward the main hesitation, the claims to confirm and the customer situation.
2. **Reference:** follow finding-references, searching for videos that show that viewer's situation and answer that hesitation or make that selling point, including videos for the competitors the user named. When the user asks whether other creators or services repeat a structure, that is a follow-up search there. The user picks the reference; skip this step when they already chose one, or start from their saved videos and notes with managing-memory when they point to those.
3. **Evidence of the reference:** when the chosen video's opening and beats are not established by what the conversation already holds, follow starting-from-a-video.
4. **Brief:** follow writing-content-briefs with the chosen reference, the selling point or hesitation as the one point, and the action from the request as the goal.

Each step's skill owns its rules and questions, including what it asks before it starts. Between steps, show what you found in a few lines. When a step needs a choice only the user can make, such as the hesitation to answer or the reference, ask and wait; otherwise continue without asking for approval. When the user asks to keep a video with a note on why they chose it, follow managing-memory, then continue.

## Questions

Batch what is open at each step into one message, and never ask again for something the conversation already holds.

## Finish

The work is finished when the brief is delivered. Filming, handing it to a creator, reviewing what they submit and counting results are the user's next steps, not steps of this skill.

After the brief skill's output, add a few lines, never as new brief fields: what the user does next (posting, preparing what the call to action needs), the action to watch, where and for how long to check it, and what to bring back. Use the source and period the user gave; do not set a period yourself, and leave an undecided one undecided rather than holding the brief. When the call to action differs from the final goal, such as recipe-request comments when the goal is purchases, name both and ask the user to bring back each count separately. Never add comments, clicks and purchases into one conversion count.

Name any hesitation, claim or reference beat that stayed unconfirmed.

## Anti-Patterns

- Running customer research, a search and evidence collection when the user's inputs already allow the brief.
- Choosing the reference for the user.
- Merging every step's findings into one long report instead of a brief.
